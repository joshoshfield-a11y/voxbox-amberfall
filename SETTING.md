## The blend, in one sentence

One contiguous open world with **soft gates, never hard locks**:
structure lives in three progression tracks the player can see and
choose between; freedom lives in the rule that nothing in the world is
ever closed to them — danger, not a door, is the gate.

## Setting: Amberfall

The world is stuck at dusk. Decades ago the Amberfall happened: the sky
broke and amber — solidified light — rained down. The old kingdom
shattered into regions, each holding out around amber light. Amber is
the one resource: it powers camps, tempers gear, and attunes people to
the wild zones. The amber glow is already the game's signature accent;
here it becomes the fiction too.

The player is a salvager — someone who walks the dark between lit
places, recovers amber, and can attune to it. No chosen-one machinery
beyond that: attunement is a skill the player builds, not a birthright
the story hands them.

## The three progression tracks (the structure)

1. **The Amber Spine (main quest).** Each region holds one beacon —
   a ruined spire. Lighting a region's beacon is its story arc: a
   short chain of quests (find the spire, clear what's nesting in it,
   bring the amber, light it) completable in any order within the
   region. Lighting a beacon unlocks that region's fast travel and
   marks the region "held." Regions can be taken in any order; the
   spine never blocks anything else.
2. **Camp tiers (the backbone).** Camps level up from salvage and
   gathering done anywhere in the world. Tier-ups unlock crafting
   recipes, loadout slots, and companion capacity. This is the track
   that rewards pure wandering: every hour spent exploring freely
   feeds the same progression as questing.
3. **Free play (the world itself).** Radiant quests, dynamic events,
   world bosses, factions, and the bestiary are available from the
   start with no gating. A world boss can be attempted at any time —
   including badly underprepared.

All three reuse systems already in the tree (quests story/side/radiant/
timed + waypoints, camp, factions, dynamic events, world bosses,
fast travel, save). New work is content and tuning, not new systems.

## Freedom guarantees (hard rules, not vibes)

- **No hard locks.** No level-gated doors, no invisible walls, no
  quest-required keys to enter a region. A fresh character can walk
  into the hardest region and get wrecked. That is allowed.
- **Danger is the gate, and it is telegraphed.** Hazard zones, enemy
  tiers, and amber corruption are visible before they are lethal
  (grading, signage in the world, bestiary entries). Dying to them is
  a decision the player made with open eyes.
- **Retreat is always free.** Camp recall works from anywhere, costs
  nothing, and never fails. Exploration is risk the player opts into.
- **The spine never blocks side content.** No quest, companion, or
  region requires finishing the main chain first.
- **Fast travel is earned by visiting**, not by quest completion.
  Arrive somewhere once, and you can return.

## First region: Emberwood

Ancient forest ruins at dusk — the flavor the engine's default spec
already carries ("a heroic knight adventure through ancient forest
ruins at dusk"), now made a real place. One region, the full loop:

- **Hollow Camp** — the first camp, the tutorial home. Campfire,
  crafting, first companion met here.
- **The Drowned Spire** — Emberwood's beacon ruin, overgrown, holding
  the region's amber. The spine's arc: reach it, clear it, light it.
- **The Amberwood wilds** — gathering grounds (amber shards, salvage),
  hazard pockets (corruption pools), a wandering mini-boss.
- **Capstone, optional:** an amber-corrupted beast nests near the
  spire. Beating it is the region's hardest fight and its best loot —
  and entirely skippable. Leaving the region never requires it.

First playable slice (plan Phase 5) is Emberwood on foot: explore,
fight, gather, craft, camp, quest, save — every system touched once,
nothing placeholder.

## What this decides, and what it doesn't

Decided: setting, world structure, the three tracks, the freedom
rules, the first region and its contents. Not decided: region count
beyond Emberwood, faction names, the companion's name, any numbers
(quest counts, tier costs, enemy stats) — those are tuning calls made
when the content is written, with the systems' existing numbers as
the starting point.
